# where-the-manifest-reading-lives

Which crate reads a manifest off the disk with its layers, what stays a
contract, and what the tool descriptor gains in the same round.

The intent: every manifest of these tools is read by one owner, and a tool
gets the layers, the meta table and the predicates by taking that owner, with
no reader of its own.

## The question

The locked design of 202609041058 makes `renki-config` the contracts: `no_std`,
no alloc, it reads no file and no environment. The TOML store and the reading
of the two files are in `renki`, the launcher library. Layering needs a
directory listing, globbing over the disk, reading every layer's text, the
environment and, for a shell predicate, a process. None of that is `no_std`.

Three places it could go:

1. A `std` half of `renki-config`, behind a feature. Changes a locked design,
   and the crate stops being what its name and readme say: the contracts.
2. The `renki` crate does discovery and layering. Every user then takes the
   launcher library: its build cache, its pin, its exec. homma's and
   mockspace's launchers already take it, but their engines, muisti, shook and
   a tool with no launcher would take a launcher to read a file. That is one
   crate holding two things, and the reason `renki-dirs` was split out of it.
3. A new crate beside the two. One more release to cut, and the TOML store
   moves into it out of `renki`.

## Decision

Option 3: a crate `renki-manifest`, layer L1 beside `renki`, `std`.

- It holds what reads a manifest off the disk: finding the layers of
  `name.ext`, following `meta.overlays.include`, reading every layer's text,
  answering a predicate from the environment or a process, merging the
  layers into one view, and the TOML store, moved here from `renki::config`
  with its writer.
- `renki-config` stays the contracts and grows the parts that are logic and
  no i/o: the layer's name parsed from a file name, the order, the `meta`
  schema read through any `Store`, the predicate as a type and its evaluation
  over a caller's answers, the glob match, the merge rule, the stacked lookup
  the resolver reads, and the layer in the provenance. Each is the one owner
  of its rule, so the typed settings path and the manifest path cannot read a
  layer two ways.
- `renki` takes `renki-manifest` for its two configuration files and keeps
  the `--cfg` flag, the variables handed to the engine and the `config` verbs.
  Its `config::toml` module goes; the path is `renki_manifest::Toml`. No alias
  is kept (pre-1.0).
- A user that only reads a manifest (an engine, muisti, shook, a tool with no
  launcher) takes `renki-manifest` and `renki-config` and nothing of the
  launcher.

Why not 2: what a crate is for decides where a thing lives, and the launcher is
for launching. Why not 1: a locked design changes only for a reason the layers
do not give, since the logic half fits the contracts as they stand and the
i/o half is exactly what they exclude.

## The descriptor's settings, and who and moment

`tool.toml` is `renki::extension::Descriptor`, which refuses unknown fields, so
what a host wants a tool to declare is renki's to add. The homma pre-work
report (step 3) wants each tool to say who it is for and the moment it fires,
and a tool to declare settings the way a launcher's `Tool` does.

- `who`: a list of words naming whom the tool is for. renki keeps them and
  interprets none; a host matches them against its own roles.
- `moment`: one line saying when the tool is reached for. Text, kept and shown.
- `settings`: rows of `key`, `kind`, `default`, `scope` and `doc`, the shape
  of `renki-config`'s `Setting`, read from TOML. The kinds offered are the
  ones whose parsers need nothing but the text: `bool`, `int`, `text`,
  `path`, `list` of text. A `choice` needs its options at run time and the
  shipped `Choice` takes them from a type, so a descriptor's `choice` is
  refused by name until a ticket gives it a run-time option list.
- To carry rows read at run time, `Declared` takes a lifetime for its strings,
  `Declared<'a, St>`; a launcher's static table is `Declared<'static, St>`.
  The parsers stay function pointers chosen from the kind's name.
- Where a tool's values come from is the host's: it resolves the rows over the
  documents it names, through the same resolver, and hands the tool the
  resolved values as `<SHORT>_CFG_<KEY>` with the descriptor's `name` as the
  short name, the way the launcher hands its engine.

## The TOML store as it moves

The store in `renki::config::toml` parses all of TOML through `toml_edit` and
answers less: an array of tables and a float come back `Isnt` (`toml.rs:214-227`),
`keys` skips arrays of tables (`:229-241`), a list's string items keep their
escapes undecoded (FIXME at `:306`), and the writer treats a `[[x]]` header as
no section (`:139`). A manifest is mostly arrays of tables, so the store that
moves into `renki-manifest` answers them, decodes its items, and its writer
knows `[[x]]`; one parser, `toml_edit`, serves the store, the merge and the
schema's deserialising, so a manifest is never parsed two ways.

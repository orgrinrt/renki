# renki-config-contracts-and-mockspace-adoption

The round that brings this repository under mockspace and adds the third crate,
`renki-config`, which is the contracts a tool's configuration is read and
written through. The design it descends from is the workspace's
`.shared/designs/renki-configuration.md`, part two: a schema declared on the
tool, a reader with a fixed precedence and provenance, a writer that keeps the
person's comments, and a `config` query every launcher answers.

## What op said, and in which order

Contracts first. The kinds, the scopes and the store are traits, with the
typestate designed before any impl: marker types and ZSTs for the kinds and the
scopes, the logic in generics with real bounds and associated types, and the
impls slotting in at the use site. TOML is the first store; anyone can write
another. A draft that put `String`, `Vec`, `Result` and `Option` into the
contracts was refused on sight: the contracts are `no_std` and no alloc, borrow
from the caller's text, and speak notko's `Outcome` and `Maybe`, the way
`renki-dirs` already does.

A lint enforces that, blocking the commit. The stack lint pack already carries
`no-std`, `no-alloc`, `no-bare-result` and `no-bare-option`; what it lacked was
a way to bind them to the two `no_std` crates and not the launcher, which is
`std` by nature. mockspace gained `crates` and `exempt_crates` under
`[lints.<name>]` for that, on its `dev` now, and this repository's
`mockspace.toml` binds the pack's stack lints to `renki-dirs` and
`renki-config` and its general ones to every crate.

The workspace rule that recorded renki as deliberately mockspace-free carried
no reason. Op's reading was laziness, and the one reason that could be
constructed, that the `cargo-mock` launcher is thin over renki, is not a build
circle: the gate is a separate binary reading this tree. So the crates moved
under `mock/crates/` where the walk reads them, the root `Cargo.toml` became
the workspace manifest, and the root readme stays the launcher crate's.

On the borrowed `&'a str` the two crates take from the caller's environment
text, and the plain integers the config kinds count in, the pack's answer is
`hilavitkutin_str::Str` and arvo's types. Op's decision: that is the long-term
shape, and not this round's. Every such site carries a `lint:allow` with the
reason and a `FIXME:` naming the port, static strings included; nothing is
ported here, and the port is a later unit for somebody else, being a matter of
consistency rather than soundness.

## What this round changes

- The repository takes mockspace: `mockspace.toml`, the crates under
  `mock/crates/`, the generated agent tree ignored, the header tool's globs
  and the header-coverage test following the move.
- `renki-config`: the contracts. `Kind` with `Bool`, `Int`, `Text`, `PathText`,
  `List<K>` and `Choice<C>`; `Scope` with `User`, `Repo` and `Either`;
  `Setting<K, S>` and the erased `Declared` row a `Tool` carries; `Store`,
  the backend contract; `Source` and the resolver over any store. Borrowed
  values throughout, `Outcome` and `Maybe` for fallibility.
- renki: the TOML store, the reader that reads the files and the environment,
  the line-based writer that keeps comments, `<SHORT>_CFG_<KEY>` and
  `<SHORT>_CONFIG` handed to the engine, and `widget config path|schema|get|set|edit`.
- renki: a retry of a failed `cargo install`, once, after a prerequisite check
  for `cargo` on `PATH`, since the failure that prompted the ruling was a
  registry deleted under the build and the second attempt succeeded alone.
- The pack's findings on the launcher that are the launcher's to fix: inline
  emphasis in rustdoc, two test files over the size limit, and the concrete
  `Vec` returns the trait-first lint names, each taken to `impl Iterator` or a
  sink where the caller is ours and allowed with a reason where the shape is
  the standard library's.
